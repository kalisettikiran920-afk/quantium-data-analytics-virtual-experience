## 📐 The Pyramid Principle Framework

The Pyramid Principle is a top-down communication structure developed by Barbara Minto at McKinsey & Company, and is the standard framework used in real Quantium client reports.

```
        ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
        │      GOVERNING THOUGHT          │  ← Single, clear recommendation
        │  "Roll out the new layout"      │    stated at the top
        └────────────────┬────────────────┘
                         │
         ┌───────────────┼───────────────┐
         │               │               │
    ┌────┴────┐     ┌────┴────┐    ┌─────┴────┐
    │Customer │     │ Brand   │    │  Trial   │  ← Key lines of argument
    │Insights │     │Insights │    │ Results  │    supporting the recommendation
    └────┬────┘     └────┬────┘    └─────┬────┘
         │               │               │
    ┌────┴────┐     ┌────┴────┐    ┌─────┴────┐
    │Life     │     │ Kettle  │    │ 3 Stores │  ← Data and evidence
    │Spending │     │ leads   │    │ all won  │    at the bottom
    └─────────┘     └─────────┘    └──────────┘
```

### Why This Matters for a Business Audience
A technical analyst thinks **bottom-up** — start with data, build to a conclusion. An executive thinks **top-down** — start with "what do I need to do?", then ask "why?" The Pyramid Principle mirrors the executive's mental model:

1. **Lead with the answer** — state the recommendation first, not last
2. **Group supporting arguments** — cluster evidence into 3–5 logical pillars
3. **Support with data** — only include data that directly supports a point
4. **One idea per slide** — each slide carries a single, clear message in its title

### Slide 4 — Business Problem

The slide frames the two strategic decisions Julia needed the data to answer:

2. Whether to roll out the new store layout network-wide?

---

### Slide 5 — Customer Insights: Life Stage
*"Older households drive over half of category sales."*

| Life Stage | Total Sales |
|---|---|
| Older Singles/Couples | $402K |
| Retirees | $366K |
| Older Families | $352K |
| Young Families | $316K |
| Young Singles/Couples | $260K |
| Midage Singles/Couples | $184K |
| New Families | $50K |

**Finding:** Older Singles/Couples, Retirees, and Older Families together account for **58% of category sales**. New Families is the smallest and lowest-penetration segment.

**Recommendation on slide:** Protect loyalty investment in older-household segments.

---

### Slide 6 — Customer Insights: Spending Segment
*"Mainstream and Budget shoppers drive category volume."*

| Spending Segment | Total Sales |
|---|---|
| Mainstream | $750K |
| Budget | $676K |
| Premium | $506K |

**Finding:** Mainstream and Budget shoppers together account for **74% of total category sales**. Older Families on a Budget is the single highest-value segment at **$168K**.

**Recommendation on slide:** Build value-focused bundles, not premium-tier campaigns.

---

### Slide 7 — Customer Insights: Brand Preference
*"Kettle is the clear category leader."*

| Brand | Total Sales |
|---|---|
| Kettle | $390K |
| Doritos | $240K |
| Smiths | $224K |
| Pringles | $177K |
| Infuzions | $99K |

**Finding:** Kettle outsells Doritos (the #2 brand) by **63%**. Classic flavours — Cheese and Original — lead sales within the Kettle range.

**Recommendation on slide:** Protect Kettle's shelf space and stock availability.

---

### Slide 8 — Trial Store Analysis: Sales Uplift
*"All three trial stores beat their control stores."*

Month-by-month sales uplift vs. matched control store (Feb–Apr 2019):

| Store | February | March | April |
|---|---|---|---|
| Store 77 | -3.7% | +39.9% | **+66.1%** |
| Store 86 | +2.5% | +27.7% | +0.4% |
| Store 88 | **+25.8%** | **+47.9%** | **+35.8%** |

**Finding:** Store 88 was the most consistent; Store 77 had the strongest late-trial peak; Store 86 showed the weakest and most uneven results.

**Recommendation on slide:** Prioritise rollout to stores matching 77 and 88.

---

### Slide 9 — Trial Store Analysis: Root Cause
*"Growth came from more visits, not bigger baskets."*

Month-by-month customer count uplift vs. matched control store:

| Store | February | March | April |
|---|---|---|---|
| Store 77 | +6.1% | +25.0% | **+56.7%** |
| Store 86 | +9.4% | +15.9% | +12.6% |
| Store 88 | 0.0% | +17.5% | +22.3% |

**Finding:** Customer counts rose at every store across all three months. The driver of sales uplift was **more people visiting the store**, not customers spending more per visit. Average transactions per customer remained roughly flat.

**Recommendation on slide:** Pair the rollout with basket-building promotions.

---

### Slide 10 — Recommendations
*"Act now to capture the value the data has identified."*

Structured as a three-horizon action plan:

| Immediate | Medium-term | Future Monitoring |
|---|---|---|
| Extend the layout to stores like 77 and 88 | Add basket-building promotions to the new layout | Track rollout stores for two full quarters |
| Protect Kettle's shelf space and stock levels | Investigate Store 86's weaker performance | Re-test the layout in a wider set of stores |
| Launch value bundles for Mainstream and Budget shoppers | Build loyalty campaigns by life stage | Monitor spend per visit as a leading indicator |

---

### Slide 11 — Conclusion
*"Roll out the new layout — the evidence supports it."*

> Category value sits with older, value-focused households, and Kettle remains critical to shelf strategy. The trial layout consistently grew sales through increased customer visits.
>
> **Recommendation:** Proceed with a phased rollout, starting with stores matching Trial Stores 77 and 88.

## ✅ Strategic Recommendations

### Immediate Actions
1. **Extend the new store layout to stores similar to 77 and 88** — these showed the strongest and most consistent sales and customer uplift
2. **Protect Kettle's shelf space and stock availability** — its 63% lead over Doritos makes it the single most critical product to keep in-stock
3. **Launch value-focused bundle promotions for Mainstream and Budget shoppers** — they drive 74% of category volume and will respond to value-based offers

### Medium-Term Actions
4. **Pair the new layout with basket-building in-store promotions** — since growth came from more visits rather than bigger baskets, targeted promotions can convert the additional footfall into higher spend per visit
5. **Investigate why Store 86 underperformed** — local competition, store format, customer mix, or layout execution may explain the muted results and could inform where not to roll out
6. **Develop life-stage-specific loyalty campaigns** targeting Older Families and Retirees, who represent the highest-value customer groups

### Future Monitoring
7. **Track rollout stores for at least two full quarters** — the trial only covered 3 months; longer-term tracking confirms gains are structural rather than novelty-driven
8. **Re-test the layout in a wider variety of store types** before committing to a full 272-store rollout
9. **Monitor spend per visit as the primary leading indicator** — if basket size starts growing alongside customer counts, it confirms the full ROI of the new layout
